title: "Vision-Based Metric Depth Estimation for Robotic Pruning"
summary: "Metric depth estimation for dormant orchard scenes, built to guide robotic pruning."
problem: >-
  A pruning robot has to know where a branch is, how thick it is, and how far
  it sits from the cutter — centimetre accuracy on thin, cluttered structure.
  Commodity depth sensors and off-the-shelf monocular models both fall apart
  there, and collecting real orchard data with ground truth at that precision
  is slow, seasonal, and expensive.
approach: >-
  Rendered a synthetic orchard in Blender: RGB, per-pixel ground-truth depth,
  masks, and full camera intrinsics and extrinsics for dormant apple trees.
  Benchmarked monocular, scale-calibrated, fine-tuned, and multi-view RGB-D
  models under one protocol — 10 trees, 5 seeds, a fixed frame budget, one
  design change at a time. The winning configuration refines fine-tuned
  monocular depth with a DINOv2 RGB+D multi-view model. Latency was profiled
  alongside accuracy so the speed/precision trade-off stays explicit.
result: >-
  Validation RMSE 0.0550 m → 0.0445 ± 0.0057 m with DINOv2 RGB+D refinement
  over 3 stereo pairs. Latency: 484.7 ms per 6-view group (80.8 ms amortized
  per depth map) vs 333.6 ms per monocular frame — more accuracy, a slower
  minimum inference unit.

  - heading: "Off-the-shelf depth broke on the branches"
    body: >-
      Depth Anything V3, metric and relative, produced unstable maps that
      dropped spurs completely. A streaming least-squares fit of scale and
      shift over trunk pixels recovered metric alignment in one constant-memory
      pass over 24k images, and preprocessing cut error further — but even the
      best calibrated model still sat at 0.138 m, far short of a cutter's
      tolerance.

  - heading: "Two refinement architectures"
    body: >-
      Both take predicted depth plus RGB from several nearby views and fuse
      them at the bottleneck. The CNN U-Net trains from scratch with shared
      encoders. The DINOv2 variant freezes a pretrained ViT-L on the RGB path
      and trains a CNN side branch for depth, because a ViT cannot simply take
      depth as a fourth channel.

    body: >-
      The depth input mattered more than the architecture: fine-tuning DA2 on

      the rest of the way. Three stereo pairs was the sweet spot — a fourth
      added nothing. Pretrained DINOv2 features beat the from-scratch CNN at
      every view count.

## Where it goes next

All of this is synthetic. The open question is whether masked fine-tuning and
multi-view refinement survive the move to real orchard images — and whether
the same models can be fine-tuned directly on real stereo pruning scenes.
