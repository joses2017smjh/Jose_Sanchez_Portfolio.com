problem: >-
  A pruning robot has to know where a branch is, how thick it is, and how far
  it sits from the cutter — centimetre accuracy on thin, cluttered structure.
  Commodity depth sensors and off-the-shelf monocular models both fall apart
  there, and collecting real orchard data with ground truth at that precision
  is slow, seasonal, and expensive.
approach: >-
  Rendered a synthetic orchard in Blender: RGB, per-pixel ground-truth depth,
  masks, and full camera intrinsics and extrinsics for dormant apple trees.
  Benchmarked monocular, scale-calibrated, fine-tuned, and multi-view RGB-D
  models under one protocol — 10 trees, 5 seeds, a fixed frame budget, one
  design change at a time. The winning configuration refines fine-tuned
  monocular depth with a DINOv2 RGB+D multi-view model, and now ships as a
  containerized service with a split ONNX graph and per-GPU latency budgets.

## What sim-to-real actually costs

Everything above is synthetic — the model has never seen a real orchard. A
hold-out affine fit on the two paper validation trees puts DA2-ft at 0.0344 m
against Blender ground truth, and a TinyUNet trunk segmenter reaches 0.894 IoU.
But running the same depth through *predicted* masks instead of ground-truth
ones moves error from 0.031 m to 0.110 m. That gap, not the headline, is the
number to beat.

Stereo fusion is not shipped: naive flow with guessed variance made things
worse, and epipolar gating left too few valid trunk pixels to score. There is
no TensorRT plan on disk, so there is no TensorRT number.
