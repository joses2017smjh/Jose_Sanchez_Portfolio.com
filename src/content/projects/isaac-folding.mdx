title: Bimanual Garment Folding in Isaac Sim
summary: >-
  Port a flow-matching VLA into Isaac Sim and score it with the challenge's own fold checker.
problem: >-
  A folding policy is easy to fool: replaying a demonstration looks like success, and a
  renderer that crashes looks like a modelling failure. The task needs a scorer nobody
  tuned, held-out garment poses, and a clear line between the policy acting and a
  demonstration being played back.
approach: >-
  Rebuilt the observation path so the policy reads three rasterised camera views and emits
  joint targets directly, with no demonstration actions in the loop. Training runs on
  18,200 rasterised frames after a measured domain-gap diagnosis traced the initial
  0-for-16 to the observation encoder, not the controller. Rollouts are graded by the
  challenge's own success_checker_garment_fold, so the pass criteria are not mine to
  adjust. The pinned Isaac Sim renderer segfaults on this cluster, so the capture path
  avoids it entirely.
contribution: >-
  I built the Isaac Sim port, the rasterised observation path, the domain-gap diagnosis,
  and the scored rollouts kept here. The "Learning to Fold" work being reproduced placed
  1st of 62 in the LeHome Challenge 2026 — that placement belongs to its authors, not to
  me. This repository reproduces their method and reports what it scores in my setup.
result: >-
  The trained policy folds 2 of 8 held-out short-pants poses, up from 0 of 16 before the
  domain-gap fix, with the checker firing at step 119 of 400 on the headline run. It scores
  0 of 4 on the other three garment classes, each of which has its own fold criteria.
  Closed-loop and reproducible, but not a solved task.

      the other arm agreed.

      proves only that the scene is solvable. Every number here comes from closed-loop
      policy rollouts, graded by the challenge's checker rather than by the renderer's own
      label. The failure clips stay published beside the successes.
