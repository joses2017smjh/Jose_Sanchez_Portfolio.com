## Why the original policy ignored the cloth

The policy was trained on path-traced frames, but the simulator rollouts feed it
rasterized ones. On identical replayed states, the converged checkpoint's
action-prediction skill (1 is perfect; 0 is no better than always predicting
the mean action) was **+0.976** on path-traced frames and **−0.038** on
rasterized frames. Fine-tuning on **18,200** rasterized frames raised it to
**+0.652**. This is a single-step, teacher-forced score, not a fold rate.
[Job ledger, rows 21155266, 21155267, 21192053 and 21193835](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/IsaacSimFolding/blob/main/SLURM_JOBS.md).

## A silent optimizer defect, found in the checkpoint bytes

The trainer ran AdamW directly on bf16 action-expert weights with no FP32 master
copy, so most updates rounded to zero. A byte-level comparison of the stored
weights showed that only **11.5%** of the 96.6M expert weights had changed. A
later fine-tune with FP32 master weights changed **59.4%**. The two checkpoints
come from different runs and steps, so this is not an isolated ablation, and
weight movement is not fold success.
