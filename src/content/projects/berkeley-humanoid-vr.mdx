title: "Berkeley Humanoid Lite: Quest VR Teleoperation"
summary: "Quest 2 controller poses drive simulated and physical humanoid arms, with feedback inside the headset."
problem: >-
  A headset demo needs a complete path to the robot: tracked poses, inverse kinematics,
  calibrated joint commands, motion gating, and feedback. My self-built Berkeley Humanoid
  Lite also needed an interface that matched its actual arm wiring and torque limits.
approach: >-
  Replaced the upstream Vive input with Quest 2 WebXR. A Python bridge sends controller
  poses to Pink/Pinocchio IK and SocketCAN arm drivers. The same interface runs with an
  offline joint model or physical arms. Added joint-specific gravity feedforward, held-grip
  motion gating, calibration panels, and camera/lidar/IMU feedback. Per-eye monocular
  relative depth and optical flow are operator overlays, not metric stereo or control inputs.
contribution: >-
  Built my physical Berkeley Humanoid Lite and calibrated its ten arm joints, then
  implemented the Quest interface, bridge, arm supervisor, tuning, diagnostics, and sensor panels. The robot
  design and underlying low-level platform are Berkeley's open-source work.
result: >-
  Physical arm teleoperation is visible in the supplied October 1 montage. The September
  24 report marks all ten arm joints OK; one wrist reaches 90% of ±10° step commands
  in 0.23–0.25 s. This demonstrates simulation-to-hardware integration, not learned
  locomotion-policy transfer or a general manipulation success rate.

sections:
  - heading: One interface, two backends
    body: >-
      The offline arm model includes joint PD control, gravity, friction, torque limits,
      and end stops. Hardware uses the as-built CAN map and per-joint calibration.
      This makes rehearsal and software checks possible before commanding the arms.
      Simulation measurements are separate from the physical step-test report.
  - heading: Feedback for the operator
    body: >-
      The headset shows stereo camera views, lidar and IMU status, and per-eye relative-depth
      and RAFT flow overlays. Relative depth is rescaled per image. CPU vision updates
      slowly and stays outside the arm-control loop. Grip gating and damping are software
      controls; they do not establish physical emergency-stop performance.
