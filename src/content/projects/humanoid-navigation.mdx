## Mapping and planning on the same control interface

The robot integrates lidar returns into a log-odds occupancy grid and replans
with A*. A reactive depth brake slows translation near obstacles or stale
packets. The learned gait receives forward-velocity and yaw-rate commands.
The simulator supplies localization and the goal coordinate.
[System and maze protocol](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/docs/RANDOM_MAZE.md).

## Compare outcomes on matched runs

A separate October 8 command-filter study recorded **259/288 goals versus
247/288** for the baseline, with **0 versus 9 falls**, across three actors.
Wall contacts remain, and shared layouts are clustered observations. This is a
different study from the 12-layout humanoid demonstration.
[Confirmation report](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/docs/H1_NAVIGATION_CONFIRMATION_2026-10-08.md).

## Sensor accuracy and navigation success are separate tests

Sensor studies cover packet loss, pose errors, IMU delay, native estimators,
and processing schedules. In the October 12 scheduling study, LiDAR–IMU clean
goals changed from **0/12 to 11/12**, while stereo remained **0/12** under both
schedules. The report retains timing, execution-order, and validation limits.
[Schedule results](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/docs/PROCESSING_SCHEDULE_RESULTS_2026-10-12.md).

The [matched IMU-history and stereo-pose studies](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/docs/H3_H4_CAMPAIGN_2026-10-08.md)
also retain negative findings. Better sensor measurements alone do not establish
better closed-loop navigation.
