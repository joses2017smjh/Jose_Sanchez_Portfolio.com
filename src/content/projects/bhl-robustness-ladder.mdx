title: "Humanoid Robustness Ladder"
summary: "How much disturbance an 11.3 kg 3D-printed humanoid can take before it stops learning."
problem: >-
  An 11.3 kg 3D-printed humanoid with 6 Nm joints has very little authority to
  arrest a disturbance, so asking how much it can take is not rhetorical.
  Upstream ships flat-ground locomotion with no curriculum and no way to score
  a policy at all — its sim2sim script needs a gamepad and a GUI, and emits no
  metric of any kind.
approach: >-
  Train in Isaac Lab on PhysX, score in MuJoCo — a different simulator on
  purpose, because a policy that only works where it trained has learned PhysX,
  not locomotion. Every randomization range upstream ships as three named
  presets was rewritten as one continuous scale, turning the presets into an
  axis you can sweep. The scoring harness drives policies through upstream's
  own controller, so observation construction is bit-identical to the sim2real
  deployment path rather than a reimplementation. Six commanded velocities,
  five seeds, 10 s episodes, one fixed terrain seed, identical for every policy.
result: >-
  89 policies trained, 6,348 scored sim2sim episodes, 288 rendered rollouts.
  Transfer inverts the training ranking: the highest-reward policy falls 23% of
  the time in MuJoCo, the shipped default 0%. Terrain training gives 11x fewer
  falls at d=1.0, and arms drop falls from 37.8% to 11.7%.

      competence-gated curriculum does far better, but lifting its safety cap
      showed the reported 0.87 m/s ceiling was an artifact of that cap:
      uncapped, it oscillates between 0 and 1.8 m/s and never converges.

      hypotheses in one sentence. A perfect height map of the ground underfoot
      lands exactly on the blind curve, leaving torque. Looking ahead does move
      it, and ray-cast depth costs 1.6% of throughput.

## Where it goes next

Colour needs the RTX renderer, which segfaults on this cluster inside the Hydra
engine, so everything here is depth-only. A newer Isaac Sim already survives the
call that kills 5.1 — it builds as a second environment beside the locked stack
rather than replacing it, because every number above came from 5.1 and none of
them should move to find out whether a renderer works.
