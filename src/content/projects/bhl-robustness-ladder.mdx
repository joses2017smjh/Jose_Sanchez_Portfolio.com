## Training reward and transfer can disagree

The flat-ground study covers five settings and three trained policies per setting.
Default randomization transfers better than the unrandomized configuration even
when training reward suggests a different ranking. The **0/360 versus 77/360**
comparison belongs to the 12-DoF biped study, not to the new full-body clip.
[Confirmation evidence](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/campaigns/20261006-confirmatory/results/dr_verdict.json).

## Turning improves; the combined recipe still fails

Actor-visible gait-clock input enables turns across three training seeds. Only
one also passes straight-walk and push qualification; the recipe requires two.
Critic-only input qualifies zero. The October 2 verdict retains that failure.
[Qualification record](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/results/repo-gpu-20260923/turngait-r12-20261001/verdict/R1.json).

## Corrected stereo pose and a retracted ice claim

Isaac Lab 3.0 read camera quaternions as `(x, y, z, w)` after they were written
`(w, x, y, z)`. Correcting the order pointed stereo down at the terrain. The
earlier ice-hazard result is retracted: patches spawned a median 72 m away.
The [findings ledger](https://github.com/joses2017smjh/bhl-robustness-ladder/blob/main/docs/FINDINGS.md)
preserves both corrections.

These are simulation results, not hardware validation: learned locomotion has not transferred to hardware.

The robot design and upstream controller are from
[Berkeley Humanoid Lite](https://github.com/HybridRobotics/Berkeley-Humanoid-Lite).
