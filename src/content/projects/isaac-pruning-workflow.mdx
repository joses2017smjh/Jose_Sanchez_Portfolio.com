track: vision
title: Vision-Guided Pruning
summary: Follow a branch with wrist RGB-D, verify contact and geometry, then release a simulated spur.
problem: >-
  A pruning arm must keep a thin branch in view and stop when tool occlusion or lost
  tracking makes release unreliable. A convincing recording alone cannot establish
  that the cut conditions were met.
approach: >-
  Built a wrist RGB-D controller in Isaac Sim using Lucas–Kanade tracking and bounded
  Cartesian commands. Dual ToF, contact, and geometry checks gate a rigid-spur release;
  a separate grader checks the saved sequence. Depth is simulator ground truth, and
  branch identity comes from mesh metadata. This is a control study, not a learned
  detector or a wood-fracture simulation.
contribution: >-
  I built the capture loop, RGB-D controller, release gates, independent grader, and
  success/failure recordings. The robot, orchard meshes, and sensor frames come from
  pinned upstream sources.
result: 'October 2: depth-aware appearance checks complete 4/4 targeted low-sun trials while rejecting
  genuine occlusion controls. A separate valid tree1 population passes 2/7. ROS 2 replay reproduces
  199/199 decision states from one capture. Simulator ground-truth depth and rigid-piece release.'
