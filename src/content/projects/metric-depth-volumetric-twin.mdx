  Semi-automated offside systems turn broadcast footage into metric 3D
  positions, but the published research stops at a monocular ball baseline

  only way to know whether an improvement is real or just a different

approach: >-
  Anchored everything to SoccerNet-v3D (CVPR CVSports 2025) because it
  publishes frozen splits, weights and metrics. Rebuilt the loader and
  camera geometry from the release files, reproduced the detection and
  monocular localization baselines, then attacked the depth error with a
  gravity-constrained trajectory fit — solving a ballistic arc over a
  360 ms window of one camera's 2D track instead of inferring depth from
  blur-inflated apparent ball size.

  Baselines reproduced within 0.003 AP (0.813 vs 0.81 published) and
  reprojection matched to a median of 0.002 px across 4,051 frames.
  Physics-constrained depth cut median error 3.26 m to 2.82 m and returned
  5.4x more estimates inside 2 m. 12/12 tests pass.

## Where it goes next

Track 5 is the one that turns "reproducing a paper" into "recreating SAOT":
lifting players, not just the ball, by triangulating keypoints from the same
synced replay pairs and fitting SMPL bodies — scored as MPJPE against
WorldPose ground truth.

![Ballistic window vs size prior on ISSIA-3D](/media/twin/track2-physics.png)

Uncertainty-aware triangulation (Track 4) runs underneath all of it:
propagating per-detection pixel noise into a 3x3 covariance, so the system
can say how much to trust each 3D point instead of reporting a bare number.
